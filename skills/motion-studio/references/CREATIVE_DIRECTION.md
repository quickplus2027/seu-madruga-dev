# Creative Direction and Anti-Repetition Lab

## Creative diagnosis (before asking users generic questions)
Inspect existing videos/assets if authorized and identify repeated pattern across:
1. Opening hook and visual grammar
2. Scene sequencing and information hierarchy
3. Camera motion / zoom direction
4. Typography and line breaking
5. Transition motifs and pacing
6. Audio cues, beats and pauses
7. Color/lighting treatment
8. Ending and CTA placement

When no prior videos are available, don't claim to know what repeats. Offer clearly divergent starting concepts and ask only for the missing creative preference.

## Mandatory 3-concept pitch for ambiguous new campaigns
Provide exactly three **structurally different** routes, e.g.:
- **Editorial kinetic type:** bold statement-led motion with typography as primary actor and disciplined cuts.
- **Product journey:** guided in-product demonstration with camera choreography and controlled focus.
- **Visual metaphor / cinematic narrative:** objects, compositing and narrative reveal with sparse explanatory text.

For each: state hook (first 2-3s), emotional tone, 3-5 beats, camera style, transition language, typography voice, audio signature and differentiated CTA. Avoid calling a color swap a different concept.

## Anti-template rules
- Don't start every video with logo scale-up + slow zoom + title.
- Don't put the same card-grid sequence in every promo.
- Alternate storytelling grammar: contrast, reversal, transformation, question/answer, reveal, physical metaphor, interface demo, character journey, data proof.
- Use pauses and negative space intentionally; complex motion only where it improves meaning.
- Distinguish reuse of engineering infrastructure from reuse of audience-facing visual ideas.
- For version variants, specify what remains and what changes (not only CTA color).

## Motion vocabulary with purpose
Kinetic typography; camera parallax; match cuts; whip-pan (when suitable); masked reveals; morphing; tracked annotations; data-driven visualization; optical rhythm; foreground/background layering; speed ramps; motion blur (when technically appropriate); depth cues; Lottie/3D only when justified. Every effect gets a purpose, entry, peak, exit and performance cost.

## Review questions
Can a person explain the video's first message without sound? Is first-frame hierarchy clear? Does any text become unreadable? Is pace too homogeneous? Does the motion support the argument? Is there a memorable signature? Does this differ visibly from prior approved work?
